\lecture[Banach--Alaoglu]{}

We did not give examples of weak-* topologies, so let us do that now.

\begin{example}
Let $X=H$ be a Hilbert space.
Then we can identify the dual space $H'\iso H$ by Riesz representation theorem.
Under this identification, $w=w^{*}$ (the weak and weak-* topologies
are the same). That is, $x_{a}\xrightarrow{w}x$ converges in the weak
topology if and only if for all $y\in H$ we have
$(x_{\alpha},y)\to(x,y)$ [this describes the weak topology, and it
  coincides with the weak-* topology thanks to Riesz].

Let $\{e_{n}\}$ be an orthonormal system. Then for every $x\in H$, we
see $(x,e_{n})\to0$. These $(x,e_{n})$ are Fourier coefficients. The
Riesz projection theorem asserts
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n}|(x,e_{n})|^{2}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Therefore, we conclude $e_{n}\xrightarrow{w}0$ weakly converges to
zero. In particular, 0 is in the weak closure of the unit spehere
[more generally, the closed unit ball is the weak closure of the unit sphere].
\end{example}

\begin{example}
Weak convergence does not generally imply normed convergence. There is
one case where they coincide: $x_{\alpha}\to x$ normed convergence if
and only if $x_{\alpha}\to x$ weak convergence and $\|x_{\alpha}\|\to\|x\|$.
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
\forwardproof\ Obvious.

\backwardproof\ We see [assuming $\RR=\FF$ is the field]
\begin{equation}
(x-x_{\alpha},x-x_{\alpha})=\|x\|^{2}+\|x_{\alpha}\|^{2}-2(x,x_{\alpha})
\end{equation}
then looking at the convergence of the right-hand side gives us the
convergence
\begin{equation}
(x-x_{\alpha},x-x_{\alpha})\to2\|x\|^{2}-2(x,x)=0.
\end{equation}
So $x-x_{\alpha}\to0$ which gives us normed convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same is true for $X=\ell^{p}$ when $1<p<\infty$.
\end{remark}

\begin{xca}
For $X=\ell^{1}$, we have $x_{n}\xrightarrow{w}x$ weakly iff $x_{n}\to x$
in the normed topology. Note that here $x_{n}$ are \emph{sequences}
(\textbf{\emph{not nets!!!}}).
\end{xca}

\begin{node}
The statement [in the previous exercise] is is not true if we consider
nets, because the weak topology is not the same as the normed topology for
$\ell^{1}$. Metrizability plays a (surprisingly?) critical role here.
\end{node}

\begin{recall}[Tychonov's theorem]
If $X_{\lambda}$ is a compact topological space [rather, a
  \emph{family} of compact topological spaces indexed by $\lambda\in\Lambda$],
then $\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}$ is compact (in the product
topology, of course).
\end{recall}

\begin{node}
This follows from Zorn's Lemma; Tychonov's theorem is one of the most
important results in point-set topology.
\end{node}

\begin{theorem}[Banach--Alaoglu]
Let $X$ be a normed vector space, let $X'$ be its dual space [with its
  usual operator normed topology]. Consider the closed unit ball
$B'\subset X'$, $B'=\{f\in X'\mid \|f\|\leq1\}$. Then $B'$ is compact
in the weak-* topology.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Caltech students must know this theorem cold!!!
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Identify $X'$ with a subset of $\RR^{X}$ (which is the set of maps
$X\to\RR$ equipped with pointwise topology), so $f\in X'\mapsto\{f(x)\}_{x\in X}$
where $X$ now acts like an indexing set. So we're considering only the
subset of $\hom(X,\RR)$ consisting of linear and continuous maps. We
can think $f\in X'\mapsto f\colon X\to\RR$ under pointwise
convergence. Then any net in $X'$, we have $\forall x\in X\ldotp f_{\alpha}(x)\to f(x)$
[this describes $f_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{w^{*}}f$]. The conclusion:
$w*$ is the product topology restricted to $X'$.

We have $B'\subset X'\subset\RR^{X}$. We claim
\begin{equation}
B'\subset\prod_{x\in X}[-\|x\|,\|x\|]\subset\RR^{X}.
\end{equation}
For any $f\in B'$ maps to $\{f(x)\}_{x\in X}$ and
\begin{equation}
|f(x)|\leq\|f\|\cdot\|x\|\leq\|x\|
\end{equation}
since $\|f\|\leq1$, so $|f(x)|\leq\|x\|$ justifying the
$B'\subset\prod_{x\in X}[-\|x\|,\|x\|]$ claim.
Then by Tychonov's theorem, $\prod_{x\in X}[-\|x\|,\|x\|]$ is
compact. All we need to prove is that $B'$ is closed in the product topology.

Let $\{f_{\alpha}\}$ be a net in $B'$ and $f_{\alpha}\to f$ in the
product space $\RR^{X}$. We need to prove $f\in B'$. [This will prove
  $B'$ is closed in the product topology, which is equivalent to $w*$ topology.]
We need to show (1) $f$ is linear, and (2) $f$ is continuous or $\|f\|\leq1$.

\textsc{Claim 1: Linearity}. For every $x,y\in X$ and every $\alpha$,
we see $f_{\alpha}(x+y)=f_{\alpha}(x)+f_{\alpha}(y)$. Then
$f_{\alpha}(x+y)\to f(x+y)$ and $f_{\alpha}(x)+f_{\alpha}(y)\to f(x)+f(y)$ 
both by pointwise convergence, they must be equal by the uniqueness of
convergence.

\textsc{Claim 2: Continuity}. Similar argument, $\|f_{\alpha}\|\leq1$
so $\forall x\in X\ldotp|f_{\alpha}(x)|\leq1$, so $f_{\alpha}(x)\to f(x)$ pointwise,
then $|f(x)|\leq\|x\|$ giving us $\|f\|\leq1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{caution}
Banach--Alaoglu theorem \emph{does not} imply the dual space $X'$ is
compact in the weak-* topology.
\end{caution}

The second major theorem is the following:

\begin{theorem}
Suppose $X$ is a separable normed vector space.
Then the closed unit ball $B'$ [in the dual space $X'$ with the weak-* topology]
is metrizable. (But $(X',w*)$ is not necessarily metrizable.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $X$ is separable, we have a dense countable subset $\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\NN}$.
Each $x_{n}$ introduces a seminorm on $X'$:
\begin{equation}
p_{n}(f):=|f(x_{n})|,
\end{equation}
for any $f\in X'$. Define the topology on $X'$, $\topology=\topology(\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\NN})$
generated by the seminorms (so $\topology$ is locally convex
topology). Clearly $\topology$ is a weaker topology than the weak-*
topology since $w*$ generated by the seminorms $p_{x}(f)=|f(x)|$ for
\emph{all} $x\in X$.

We claim $\topology|_{B'}=w*|_{B'}$ these topologies restricted on the
unit ball coincide. If we stipulate this (and defer its proof for the
moment), then this means
\begin{equation}
\id\colon(B',\topology|_{B'})\to(B',w*|_{B'})
\end{equation}
is continuous. [This means $w*|_{B'}$ is weaker than $\topology|_{B'}$
  since it states the preimage of open subsets $U\in w*|_{B'}$ must
  have their preimage $\id^{-1}U=U\in\topology|_{B'}$,
  which means they coincide.]
We know $\topology|_{B'}$ is metrizable since it is generated by
countably many seminorms, hence the theorem.

Well, we just need to prove $\topology|_{B'}=w*|_{B'}$ these topologies restricted on the
unit ball coincide. We need to prove: [For any net $f_{\alpha}$] If $f_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\topology}f$, then $f_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{w*}f$.
Then for any $n$, $p_{n}(f_{\alpha}-f)\to0$. Then
$f_{\alpha}(x_{n})\to f(x_{n})$ for any $n\in\NN$ [by definition of $p_{n}$].
We want to show $f_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{w*}f$; i.e., $\forall x\in X\ldotp f_{\alpha}(x)\to f(x)$.
Then by the triangle inequality, we have
\begin{subequations}
  \begin{align}
|f_{\alpha}(x)-f(x)| &\leq |f_{\alpha}(x)-f_{\alpha}(x_{n})|+|f_{\alpha}(x_{n})-f(x_{n})|+|f(x_{n})-f(x)|\\
&\leq2\|x-x_{n}\|+|f_{\alpha}(x_{n})-f(x_{n})|
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
since we're on the unit disc. So, first we find $x_{n}$, then we use
pointwise convergence to make $|f_{\alpha}(x_{n})-f(x_{n})|$
sufficiently small. Then we're done.
\end{proof}
